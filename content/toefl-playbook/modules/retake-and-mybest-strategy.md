<!-- rich_text: Why a retake needs a strategy -->
Plenty of strong applicants take the TOEFL iBT more than once. A first attempt can fall short by half a band in one section because of nerves, an unfamiliar task type or a bad night's sleep. A retake can fix that, but it costs money and time, and a poorly planned retake often produces almost the same result.

This module helps you decide *whether* to retake, *when*, and *how* to prepare so that the second attempt is genuinely different. It also explains **MyBest scores**, ETS's version of a superscore, which can change your strategy completely if your target institutions accept them. The rules here come from ETS's FAQ, score pages, overview and fees page, checked on 24 October 2026. Policies can change, so confirm them on ets.org before you book.

- Explain MyBest scores and how they appear on 1–6 and 0–120 reports.
- Find out whether each target institution accepts MyBest scores.
- Decide whether a retake is worth it, using your gap, your deadline and the cause of the shortfall.
- Choose a sensible gap between attempts.
- Decide between a score review and a retake for Writing or Speaking.
- Plan a retake that targets the right sections without letting the others drop.
- Send the right scores to the right institutions.

<!-- rich_text: The official retake rules -->
ETS's FAQ sets out a short rule: you can take the TOEFL iBT **as often as you like, but not more than once in a 3-day period**.

Other rules shape every retake:

- **Each attempt is a full test.** You cannot retake a single section. ETS states that if you do not answer at least one Reading question, one Listening question, one Writing task and one Speaking task, none of your sections are scored.
- **Each attempt is a new registration** with its own test fee, which depends on your country.
- **Scores are valid for two years** from the test date. ETS will not report or send scores older than that.
- **Every attempt has its own score report**, showing that test date's scores and your MyBest scores.

In theory, you could test twice in one week. In practice, very short gaps rarely change much unless the first result was spoiled by something outside your English, such as illness or a technical problem.

<!-- rich_text: What MyBest scores are -->
When you send a TOEFL score report, it includes two sets of scores:

1. **The scores from the test date you selected**, with four section scores and an overall score.
2. **Your MyBest scores**: your highest score in each section across all your valid TOEFL iBT tests from the last two years, which may come from different test dates.

On the 1–6 scale, ETS's overview explains that the MyBest overall band reflects the **average of your highest section scores**. In other words, MyBest builds the best possible profile from your recent attempts.

ETS's FAQ is explicit that institutions receiving your scores see your test-date scores *and* your MyBest scores. You cannot send MyBest alone, and you cannot hide a test date's scores inside a report you send for that date. What you do control is *which* test date you send.

<!-- rich_text: MyBest across the 2026 scale change -->
Because the scale changed on 21 January 2026, some applicants have results on both scales. ETS's score page explains what happens:

- **If the report is for a test taken on the new 1–6 scale**, the MyBest section converts any older 0–120-scale scores to the 1–6 scale, so all your recent results can contribute.
- **If the report is for a test taken on the old 0–120 scale**, the MyBest section includes only scores from the old scale.

So if you took the test in late 2025 and again in 2026, a report for the 2026 test can include your 2025 sections in MyBest, converted to bands. The conversion follows the official comparison table between the scales, which you can find in [The 2026 score scale](/score-scale-2026).

<!-- rich_text: A worked MyBest example -->
Here is how MyBest can work in your favour.

| Section | Test 1 | Test 2 | MyBest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reading | 5.5 | 4.5 | 5.5 |
| Listening | 5 | 4.5 | 5 |
| Writing | 4 | 5 | 5 |
| Speaking | 4 | 4.5 | 4.5 |
| Average | 4.625 | 4.625 | 5.0 |
| Overall | 4.5 | 4.5 | 5 |

Neither test on its own reaches an overall 5. Both give an average of 4.625, which rounds to 4.5. But the MyBest profile, taking Reading and Listening from test 1 and Writing and Speaking from test 2, averages exactly 5.0.

We have rounded the MyBest overall in the same way as an ordinary overall score. ETS describes the MyBest overall as reflecting the average of the best section scores; check the figure on your actual report.

The example also shows the main risk: if your target institution does *not* accept MyBest, this student still has a 4.5 overall from every single test.

<!-- rich_text: Who decides whether MyBest counts -->
ETS is clear that **each institution decides** whether to accept MyBest scores. Policies vary widely: some universities accept them fully, some accept only scores from a single test date, and some accept MyBest for some programmes but not others. Scholarship programmes and visa authorities may have their own rules.

To find out:

1. **Search the institution's English-requirements page** for "MyBest", "superscore" or "single test date".
2. **Check the programme page as well**, since graduate schools and departments sometimes set their own rules.
3. **If you find nothing, ask in writing.** A short email works: *"Dear Admissions Office, I am applying to [programme] for [term]. Do you accept TOEFL iBT MyBest scores to meet your English requirement, or must all section scores come from a single test date? Thank you for your help."*
4. **Keep the reply.** If it is ever questioned, you have the institution's own words.

<!-- callout: MyBest decision rule -->
Plan as if MyBest is not accepted unless an institution says in writing that it is
If even one of your target institutions requires a single test date, your retake must reach every section target in one sitting. If all of them accept MyBest, you can focus a retake on the sections that fell short, while keeping the others in reasonable shape.

<!-- rich_text: Should you retake? A decision framework -->
Answer three questions before booking.

**1. How big is the gap?** Write down the required band and your result for each section. A gap of half a band in one or two sections is often closable with focused work. A gap of a full band or more across several sections usually needs a longer preparation period before another attempt is worth the fee.

**2. What caused it?** A shortfall caused by something temporary, such as illness, nerves, a timing mistake or one unfamiliar task type, often responds quickly to practice. A shortfall caused by limited vocabulary, slow reading or weak listening comprehension takes longer to fix.

**3. How much time do you have?** Count backwards from your earliest application deadline. Leave time for scores to be released (3 days), for institutions to receive them (ETS estimates 4–8 business days through ETS Data Manager), and ideally for one more attempt after the retake.

If the gap is small, the cause is fixable and the deadline allows, retake. If the gap is large, prepare first and set a mock-test target before you book. If the deadline is too close for any meaningful improvement, consider applying with your current scores where they meet requirements, or ask the institution about conditional admission or pre-sessional English options.

<!-- rich_text: Diagnosing why you fell short -->
Your official report and ETS account give more than five numbers. According to ETS's FAQ, your account includes your proficiency level for each section, feedback on which Reading and Listening question types you have shown you can handle and which are still developing, and a closer look at your Speaking and Writing, including language use, grammar and mechanics.

Combine that with your own memory of the test:

- **Reading:** was the problem Complete the Words spelling, the speed of the router stage, or understanding the academic passage?
- **Listening:** did you lose single-exchange items through intonation and implied meaning, or the longer talks through note-taking?
- **Writing:** did you run out of time in the email, miss a required point, or write less than 100 words in the discussion?
- **Speaking:** did Listen and Repeat break down on long sentences, or did your interview answers stop early?

Then reproduce the test conditions: take a timed mock on the [Schood TOEFL simulator](https://toefl.schood.online/full-exam) and see whether the same weaknesses appear. A weakness that shows up in both the real test and a mock is your retake priority.

<!-- rich_text: Small differences and score variation -->
Every test score has some measurement uncertainty, and the banded scale is less fine-grained than the old 0–30 scale, as ETS itself notes. Two attempts at the same level of English can differ by half a band in a section. That has two consequences.

1. **Do not over-read a half-band drop.** If one section fell by 0.5 between tests while you were preparing, that may be normal variation rather than a real decline.
2. **Do not aim exactly at the requirement.** If you need a 4.5 in Speaking and your practice average is exactly 4.5, a retake has roughly even odds of landing above or below it. Build a margin in practice before you book.

This is also why MyBest is valuable where it is accepted: it lets your best performance in each section count.

<!-- rich_text: How long to wait between attempts -->
The legal minimum is 3 days. The useful gap depends on what you need to change.

| Situation | Sensible gap |
|---|---|
| A one-off problem: illness, technical failure, panic | 1–3 weeks |
| Half a band short in one or two sections, cause clear | 3–6 weeks |
| A band short in several sections | 8–12 weeks or more |
| Large gap, limited English base | Prepare first; book when mocks reach the target |

These are planning guidelines, not ETS rules. Because official scores arrive 3 days after the test, you can usually review your full results before committing to a new date. If your deadline is close, booking a provisional second date early can secure a slot, but check the rescheduling fee and policy first.

<!-- callout: Retake timing rule -->
Book the retake when your practice results, not your hopes, reach the target
Before booking, you want at least two recent timed mocks in which every section that matters is at or above its target. If you need to hold a date because of a deadline, choose it now, but keep checking your mock results against that date.

<!-- rich_text: Retaking when MyBest is accepted -->
If every target accepts MyBest, the retake becomes a section-focused project.

1. **Identify the weak sections** that are below target, for example Writing 4 when you need 4.5.
2. **Spend most of your preparation time on them**, with targeted practice on the specific task types.
3. **Maintain the other sections** with lighter practice. You still have to sit the whole test, and a full attempt still matters to your stamina.
4. **On test day, do your best in every section.** A higher score in a strong section can still raise your MyBest overall, and some institutions may consider single-date scores too.

The trap is neglecting the strong sections so much that they collapse. That does not hurt your MyBest, but it can matter if a scholarship or a second institution looks at single-date scores.

<!-- rich_text: Retaking when MyBest is not accepted -->
If any target requires all sections from one test date, you need a balanced attempt.

- **Raise the weak sections** to their targets with margin.
- **Protect the strong sections.** If your Reading was 5.5 last time, keep regular Reading practice so it does not slip back to 5 and pull the overall down.
- **Train the full sequence.** Take full timed mocks in the right order, Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking, with no break, so your Speaking holds up at the end.
- **Fix test-day causes.** If something on the day cost you points, such as noise at the centre or a technical problem at home, change the condition. See [Test day guide](/test-day-guide).

<!-- rich_text: Don't let one section take over -->
A common pattern on retakes: a learner who needs a higher Speaking score spends every day practising interview answers and stops reading and listening entirely. The next test brings Speaking up by half a band, but Reading and Listening drop, and the overall is unchanged.

A better balance for most retake plans:

- **About 60 percent** of study time on the sections below target.
- **About 30 percent** on maintenance of the other sections.
- **About 10 percent** on full timed mocks and review.

Adjust these proportions to your situation; they are a starting point, not an ETS recommendation. Always include at least one full mock in the final two weeks.

<!-- rich_text: Planning the cost of a retake -->
Each attempt is a full registration. The test fee depends on your country, and ETS lists separate fees for services such as late registration, rescheduling, reinstating cancelled scores, extra score reports and score review. At the time of writing, ETS's fees page listed, for example, US$49 for registering within 7 days of the test, US$69 for rescheduling and US$20 for reinstating cancelled scores. ETS's score pages and fees page show different prices for additional score reports (US$25 and US$29), so check the fees page before you order.

Ways to keep costs under control:

- **Avoid late registration** by booking early once your mocks are ready.
- **Use your free score reports** for each test: every registration includes up to four.
- **Decide on score review early**, because it is not available once scores are sent.
- **Prepare properly**, since an unprepared retake costs the same as a prepared one.

<!-- rich_text: Sending scores strategically -->
Each test registration includes up to **four free score reports** to institutions you choose before the test. ETS pages give the deadline as 10 p.m. on the day of the test and as 10 p.m. local test-centre time on the day before; finish by the day before to be safe. After that, you can order additional reports through your ETS account for a fee for as long as your scores are valid.

Remember how reports work:

- **A report is for one test date** and includes that date's scores plus your MyBest scores.
- **You choose the test date** when you order a report. If your second test is better, send that one.
- **Free recipients receive this test's scores automatically** once you report them. If you are unsure how a test will go, you might choose to add recipients later as paid reports instead. That costs money but gives you control.
- **Free recipients block score review.** ETS says that if you choose any institution to receive your scores, you cannot request a review of Writing or Speaking for that test.

<!-- rich_text: Score review or retake? -->
If your Writing or Speaking score surprised you, you have two options: request a score review or retake.

**Score review**
- Available for Writing and/or Speaking only; Reading and Listening scores cannot be reviewed or appealed.
- Must be requested within 30 days of the test, for a fee. At the time of writing ETS listed US$80 for one section and US$160 for both.
- Not available if you have sent the scores to any institution, including free recipients.
- The result may stay the same.

**Retake**
- Measures you again on all four sections.
- Costs a full test fee, but also gives you a new chance at every section and new MyBest data.

A review makes most sense when a Writing or Speaking score is clearly out of line with your practice and you did not list free recipients. A retake makes more sense when several sections need to move, or when your practice results do not clearly support a higher score.

<!-- rich_text: Cancelled scores and retakes -->
If you cancelled your scores at the end of a test, they are not reported to you or anyone else. You can reinstate them through your ETS account within 60 days of the test date, for a fee, and ETS says reinstated scores are reported about three weeks after it receives the request and payment.

Two things to consider:

- **Reinstated scores can become part of your history.** Once reported, a test's scores can contribute to MyBest on later reports, within the two-year window.
- **Cancelling rarely helps a retake strategy.** Institutions see only the reports you send, and MyBest only shows your highest section scores. Unless a test went so badly that you do not want your listed recipients to see it, reporting usually keeps more options open.

<!-- rich_text: Changing format between attempts -->
You can take one attempt at a test centre and the next as the Home Edition, or the other way round. ETS describes the Home Edition as the same test with the same scoring, but check that each of your target institutions accepts it.

A format change can be part of the fix:

- **Move from home to a centre** if your first attempt suffered from technical problems, a noisy household or stress about the room rules.
- **Move from a centre to home** if noise from other test takers, travel or limited dates hurt your first attempt, and your computer, internet and room meet every ETS requirement.

Whatever you choose, take your final mocks under the conditions of the new format.

<!-- rich_text: Retake plans by available time -->
**Two weeks (one-off problem or very small gap).**
- Days 1–2: review your score report and ETS account feedback; take one timed mock to confirm the weak points.
- Days 3–10: daily targeted practice on the one or two weakest task types, plus light maintenance.
- Days 11–12: a final full mock under real conditions; review mistakes only.
- Days 13–14: rest, logistics and test-day preparation.

**Four to six weeks (half a band in one or two sections).**
- Week 1: diagnosis and a full mock; study the relevant skill modules.
- Weeks 2–4: targeted practice, with one or two mixed-section sessions each week.
- Week 5: full mock; adjust the plan.
- Week 6: final mock early in the week, light practice, test.

**Eight to twelve weeks (a band or more).** Rebuild foundations, such as vocabulary, spelling, listening volume and speaking fluency, alongside task practice, with a full mock every two to three weeks. The module [Score goals and study plans](/score-goals-and-study-plans) gives week-by-week structures.

<!-- rich_text: Avoiding a retake loop -->
Some applicants retake again and again with the same result. The cause is usually the same each time: they repeat the same preparation and hope for a different day.

Set yourself rules before the second attempt:

1. **Change something real.** A new study method for the weak section, a different test format, a different time of day, or more preparation time.
2. **Use evidence.** Book only when mock results support the target.
3. **Set a stop point.** Decide in advance what you will do if a retake falls short again, for example apply to programmes whose requirements you already meet, consider conditional admission routes, or take a longer break to build English before testing again.
4. **Watch the calendar.** Scores count for two years, but deadlines do not wait. Keep a written timeline.

<!-- checklist: Before you book a retake -->
- I know exactly which sections fell short and by how much.
- I know whether each target institution accepts MyBest scores, in writing.
- I have identified the cause of each shortfall from my report and a mock.
- I have changed my preparation for the weak sections.
- Two recent timed mocks show the target in every section that matters.
- My retake date leaves time for scores to reach institutions before my deadline.
- I have decided on score review for the previous test before sending any scores.
- I know my free score recipients for the new test.

2. Timur needs Writing 5 for a scholarship that uses single-date scores only. He scored W 4.5 and his practice emails are often missing one required point. His deadline is in five weeks. Retake or score review?
3. Dilnoza scored R 4, L 4.5, W 4, S 4 and needs 5 overall. Her deadline is in three weeks. What is realistic?

<!-- rich_text: Answers to the practice cases -->
1. Speaking is the only section below her section minimum, and her average is 4.75. Raising Speaking to 4.5 gives a MyBest profile of 5.5, 5, 4.5 and 4.5, an average of 4.875, which rounds to 5 and meets the 4.5 floor. Her retake should focus mainly on Speaking, while keeping the other sections in shape.
2. A score review only makes sense if his Writing score seems wrong; his own practice suggests a real weakness, and a review is impossible anyway if he sent scores to any institution. With five weeks, a focused **retake** is more promising: fix the missing-point problem in the email, practise the discussion task, and keep the other sections steady, because the scholarship needs all sections from one test date.
3. She is a full band short overall, which in three weeks is a lot to change. Realistically, she should either apply to programmes whose requirements she meets, ask about conditional or later-entry options, or move her plans to a later intake and prepare properly before testing again.
4. Not for that university. MyBest keeps his best Speaking score of 4.5. The drop is also within normal variation. It is still worth checking whether Speaking practice slipped during his preparation.
5. No. ETS says that if you chose any institution to receive your scores, you cannot request a Writing or Speaking review for that test.

<!-- rich_text: Frequently asked questions -->
**Is there a limit on how many times I can take the TOEFL iBT?** ETS sets no overall limit. The only rule is not more than once in a 3-day period.

**Will institutions see all my attempts?** They see the reports you send. A report contains the scores of the test date you choose plus your MyBest scores, which may include sections from other recent tests.

**Do old 0–120 scores count in MyBest?** On a report for a 1–6-scale test, ETS converts older 0–120-scale scores to the 1–6 scale in the MyBest section. On a report for an old-scale test, MyBest shows only old-scale scores.

**Does MyBest include expired scores?** No. MyBest uses valid scores from the last two years.

**Is MyBest the same as a superscore?** It works in a similar way, combining your best section scores across tests, but whether it is accepted is always the institution's decision.

**Can I retake only Speaking?** No. Every attempt is the full test, and all four sections must be attempted for any of them to be scored.
