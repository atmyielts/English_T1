<!-- rich_text: Why test day deserves its own plan -->
You can prepare for months and still lose a test on the day: a name that does not match your ID, a laptop that fails the equipment check, a phone left on the desk, a moment of looking away from the screen at home, or an empty stomach in a two-hour test with no break. None of these has anything to do with your English.

This module turns ETS's official rules into a plan you can follow. It covers:

- **both ways of taking the test**, at an authorised test centre and as the Home Edition;
- **the test itself**, from the first Reading item to the decision you make on the final screen;
- **the days after**, from unofficial scores to score reports.

The rules here come from ETS's test-day pages, equipment requirements and FAQ. Rules can change, so read the *TOEFL iBT Information Bulletin* and the ETS pages listed under the official references again in the week of your test.

<!-- checklist: What you will be able to do -->
- Prepare documents, equipment and your room or route well before the test.
- Check in smoothly at a test centre or with a Home Edition proctor.
- Avoid the behaviours that can get a Home Edition test invalidated.
- Handle technical problems the way ETS expects.
- Pace your energy through a test with no scheduled break.
- Recover quickly when one task goes badly.
- Make a sensible report-or-cancel decision at the end.
- Know exactly what happens in the days after the test.

<!-- rich_text: The shape of the day -->
Here is what the day looks like in time, based on ETS's figures.

1. **Check-in: about 30 minutes.** ETS advises arriving at a test centre at least 30 minutes early, and Home Edition check-in includes identity verification and a room scan with the proctor.
2. **The test: just under 2 hours.** Reading, then Listening, then Writing, then Speaking. ETS's approximate base times are 30, 29, 23 and 8 minutes, plus directions. Reading and Listening are adaptive, so the exact time varies.
3. **The final screens: a few minutes.** You see unofficial Reading and Listening scores and choose whether to report or cancel all your scores.

ETS suggests planning about 2.5 hours in total. Add your travel time for a test centre, or time to set up your room at home, and keep the rest of the day free. You will think more clearly if nothing else is scheduled.

<!-- rich_text: Two weeks before the test -->
Use the last two weeks for logistics as well as practice.

- **Re-read the requirements of your institutions** and confirm your section targets. See [The 2026 score scale](/score-scale-2026).
- **Check your ETS account.** The name must match your ID *exactly*. If it does not, fix it now; ETS will not let you test with a mismatch and will not refund the fee.
- **Check your ID** against the ETS ID requirements for your country and testing location. Make sure it will still be valid on the test date.
- **Add your four free score recipients.** ETS pages disagree slightly on the deadline, so finish by the day before the test.
- **Take a final full mock** on the [Schood TOEFL simulator](https://toefl.schood.online/full-exam) at the same time of day as your real test, in one sitting.
- **Home Edition:** run the TOEFL Test App's equipment check, and verify your identity in the ETS identity app if it applies to you. ETS's FAQ says to complete the ID verification at least 48 hours before the test.
- **Test centre:** plan your route and travel time, and know where you will wait if you arrive early.

From now on, practise the way you will test: no breaks, notes on a single sheet or whiteboard, phone out of reach.

<!-- rich_text: The last 48 hours -->
The last two days are for staying sharp, not for cramming.

- **Light, focused practice.** A few Complete the Words paragraphs, a set of Build a Sentence items, one email and one discussion post, and some Listen and Repeat and interview practice aloud. Stop well before the evening.
- **Re-run the equipment check** for Home Edition on the same day of the week and at the same time of day as your test, as ETS recommends, because internet bandwidth changes with time and household use.
- **Prepare the room** if testing at home, so that on the day you only have to check it.
- **One day before a test centre appointment**, sign in to your ETS account and confirm nothing has changed, such as the time or building. ETS recommends doing this 24 hours ahead.
- **Sleep.** Sleep affects attention and memory far more than one more practice set.

<!-- checklist: The night before -->
- Valid ID ready, name matching my ETS account exactly; a second ID if I have one.
- Appointment time and place confirmed in my ETS account.
- Free score recipients finalised.
- Home Edition: computer charged and plugged in, test app installed, equipment check passed, room cleared.
- Home Edition: whiteboard or blank sheet in a clear sleeve, and an erasable marker.
- Test centre: route and departure time planned with a buffer.
- Water and a light meal planned for before the test.
- Alarm set; nothing else scheduled near the test.

<!-- rich_text: At a test centre: arriving and checking in -->
Arrive at least 30 minutes before your appointment. Check-in includes showing your ID and following the centre's security procedures, which can take time when many people test at once.

**ID.** Bring valid, acceptable identification. ETS recommends bringing at least two forms of ID in case the administrator questions the first one. If you do not bring acceptable ID, or the name does not match your account exactly, you will not be allowed to test and the fee will not be refunded.

**Local rules.** Check with your centre about any local requirements, such as policies on masks.

**Instructions.** Follow the staff exactly. ETS states that if you do not follow test-centre staff instructions you will not be permitted to test, and that security violations can lead to dismissal and cancelled scores.

At the start of the test you will be given a headset with a microphone. Make sure it fits comfortably and the volume suits you before the timed sections begin.

<!-- rich_text: At a test centre: what you can and cannot bring -->
Keep it simple: if an item is not approved, do not take it into the testing room.

- **Not allowed in the room:** phones, watches, and any other electronic, recording, listening, scanning or photographic devices. These are stored with your personal belongings.
- **No food or drink** in the testing room unless you have an approved health accommodation, which must be requested before you register.
- **Notes:** the centre provides scratch paper and pencils. You may not bring your own paper or writing tools. ETS's FAQ says you can ask for more scratch paper during the test.

The complete list of prohibited items and items that may be inspected is in the *Bulletin*.

<!-- rich_text: At a test centre: working with noise -->
At a test centre, other people take the test in the same room and may not be in the same section as you. When you are reading or listening, someone near you may be speaking their answers aloud.

Prepare for this in practice:

- **Train with background speech.** For some practice sessions, play a recording of people talking at low volume while you do Reading or Listening tasks.
- **Use the headset well.** Set a volume that lets you hear the recordings clearly without straining.
- **Do not wait for silence in Speaking.** Speak at a steady, normal volume as soon as recording starts. Raising your voice or whispering does not help intelligibility.

If something genuinely disrupts your test, such as equipment failure or a serious disturbance, raise your hand and tell the staff at once. The *Bulletin* explains how to report problems with a test administration.

<!-- rich_text: Home Edition: preparing your room -->
The Home Edition is the same test as at a centre, but your room becomes the test centre, and ETS's rules for it are strict. If your environment does not meet them on the day, you may not be allowed to test, and ETS says you will not receive a refund or free reschedule.

- **Be alone** in a private room, with all doors closed. No one may enter during the test. Public places such as cafes or parks are not allowed.
- **Cover** transparent walls or doors and large open windows.
- **Clear the walls** of anything related to the test, and clear the desk and the area around it of everything not approved.
- **Use a desk or table** with your computer plugged into power, and a standard chair. You cannot sit on a bed, sofa or overstuffed chair.
- **Position yourself** so the proctor can see the door if asked.
- **No food or drink** during the test.
- **Appearance:** your ears and face must stay visible. Religious headwear is allowed if your ears remain visible. Face masks are not allowed at check-in or during the test. Remove headphones, smartwatches, jewellery and hair accessories before checking in; ETS says wedding and engagement rings can stay on.

Tell the people you live with when you are testing, and ask them to keep the internet free and the house quiet for about three hours.

<!-- rich_text: Home Edition: equipment -->
ETS's equipment rules, in short:

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Computer | Desktop or laptop only, no tablet or phone |
| Screens | One monitor only |
| Keyboard | One keyboard only |
| System | Licensed Windows 11 or later, or macOS 10.13 or later |
| Sound | Internal or external speaker; no headsets or earphones |
| Microphone | Internal or external, not part of a headset |
| Camera | Webcam that can be moved to show the room 360° |
| Second camera | If required: a charged phone or tablet (iOS 12+ or Android 8+) |

Install the TOEFL Test App from the My TOEFL Home dashboard and run its **Equipment Check**, which includes a bandwidth test. Run it several times, including at the time of day you will test. Close all browsers and applications you do not need, and pause other devices in your home that use a lot of bandwidth.

Disable any screen-sharing or remote-access software and writing tools before the test, and make sure they cannot open automatically. ETS names examples such as Zoom, Skype, TeamViewer, Microsoft Teams, AnyDesk, Apple Remote Desktop, Grammarly and spell check.

During the test you may see instructions about checking a headset. ETS says to ignore them at home, because headsets are not used or allowed in the Home Edition.

<!-- rich_text: Home Edition: check-in step by step -->
When it is time, you select **Start Test** from your order confirmation email or the My TOEFL Home dashboard, and the TOEFL Test App opens. Then:

1. **Virtual waiting room.** You see your test details and begin checking in.
2. **Second camera.** If it applies to you, you connect your phone or tablet as a second camera by scanning a QR code, and place it on a sturdy support as instructed.
3. **Identity check.** The proctor completes an assisted identity verification with your ID.
4. **Room scan.** You use your camera to show the proctor the room, the desk surface and the area below it.
5. **Final equipment check.** Just before the test starts, the proctor runs one more equipment check and helps troubleshoot if needed.

ETS states that the test timer does not start until your equipment has been checked and you begin the test, so problems at check-in do not eat into your test time. After check-in, put your phone behind you and out of reach unless it is serving as the second camera.

<!-- rich_text: Home Edition: behaviours that can invalidate your test -->
The proctor and ETS's AI monitoring watch for behaviour that could indicate cheating. Innocent habits can look suspicious on camera, so train yourself out of them in practice. ETS lists these as behaviours that can invalidate a test and lead to score cancellation:

- **Talking out loud to yourself**, including reading questions aloud. Speaking is only expected during the Speaking section.
- **Looking away from the screen** for long periods, for example staring at the ceiling while thinking.
- **Moving out of view** of the webcam or the second camera.
- **Taking a break.** There are no breaks unless approved as an accommodation.

Also: do not communicate with anyone except the proctor, and do not use any unauthorised materials, including prewritten notes, textbooks, smartwatches or other devices. ETS also says it monitors repeated reuse of the same devices and testing locations for unauthorised purposes.

In your final mocks at home, sit exactly as you will on test day: facing the screen, hands visible, thinking with your eyes on the screen or your whiteboard.

<!-- callout: When something goes wrong at home -->
Tell the proctor first; never fix a problem silently off camera
If something interrupts your test, speak and wave to get the proctor's attention, or use the chat if you are still connected. Do not stand up, leave the camera view or start unplugging cables on your own. Calm, visible communication protects your test; unexplained movement can look like a rule violation.

<!-- rich_text: Technical problems: what ETS expects you to do -->
ETS's guidance for Home Edition technical problems:

- **Before the test starts:** your equipment is checked again right before testing, and the proctor tells you about any issue so you can resolve it before the test begins.
- **During the test:** if you have a problem, speak and wave to get the proctor's attention. If you still have internet but have been disconnected from the test, use the chat.
- **Brief internet loss:** you are reconnected to the proctor automatically when the connection returns, and if the test can be reopened, the proctor reopens it.
- **No reconnection:** if you cannot reconnect to the test or the proctor, restart the check-in process.
- **Second camera disconnects:** during check-in you rescan the QR code; during the test the proctor pauses your exam and explains how to reconnect.

If you could not complete a test because of a technical problem and have not received instructions, contact TOEFL Services through the ETS contacts page. Keep notes of what happened and when.

<!-- rich_text: Notes: materials and technique -->
You can take notes in every section.

- **Test centre:** scratch paper and pencils provided by the centre.
- **Home Edition:** one small desktop whiteboard with an erasable marker, or one blank sheet of paper inside a transparent sheet protector with an erasable marker. No pens or pencils, and no paper notes outside the sleeve. At the end, the proctor asks you to erase it and show both sides to the camera.

At home, space is limited, so practise with exactly the surface you will use. Effective notes on the 2026 test are short:

- **Listening:** for talks and announcements, a few key words with arrows for cause and effect and a mark for contrasts. For short Listen and Choose a Response items, notes rarely help; listen for the speaker's intent instead.
- **Writing:** for the email, three quick labels for the three required points; for the discussion, your position and one example.
- **Speaking:** in the interview, one word for your main point and one for your example, if there is time to glance.

Notes support memory; they should never replace listening.

<!-- rich_text: A section-by-section game plan -->
**Reading (about 30 minutes, adaptive).** Start at full concentration; the first stage decides your second module. In Complete the Words, read the whole paragraph first, then fill the blanks, and check spelling and word endings before moving on. In Daily Life and Academic texts, read the question first, then find the evidence. See [Reading modules](/reading-complete-the-words).

**Listening (about 29 minutes, adaptive).** Everything plays once. Listen for purpose and attitude, not only facts. If you miss an item, choose your best answer and let it go; the next recording needs your attention. See [Listening strategy](/listening-strategy).

**Writing (23 minutes).** Build a Sentence first: about 6 minutes for ten items by our arithmetic. Then 7 minutes for the email, covering all three required points, and 10 minutes for the discussion post, aiming above the 100-word guideline with a clear position and a developed example. Leave a minute to proofread each written task.

**Speaking (about 8 minutes).** For Listen and Repeat, listen to the whole sentence, then repeat it at a natural pace in meaningful chunks. For the interview, answer directly in your first sentence, then give a reason and a concrete example, and keep talking until time runs out. See [Listen and Repeat](/speaking-listen-and-repeat) and [Take an Interview](/speaking-take-an-interview).

<!-- rich_text: Managing your energy without a break -->
The updated test has no scheduled break, and Speaking, the section where your voice and confidence matter most, comes last. Plan for that.

- **Eat a proper meal** about one to two hours before the test, and drink water, but not so much that you will need the bathroom halfway through.
- **Use the bathroom** right before check-in.
- **Reset between sections.** On the screen between sections, sit back, breathe out slowly twice, roll your shoulders and refocus. At home, stay seated and keep your eyes on the screen while you do it.
- **Warm up your voice before the test**, for example by reading a paragraph aloud at home or quietly on the way to the centre. Do not speak during the test until Speaking begins.
- **Expect a dip in Writing.** After nearly an hour of Reading and Listening, attention often fades. Your Writing plan should be automatic by now, so follow it rather than improvising.

<!-- rich_text: Handling nerves -->
Some nervousness is normal and can even sharpen attention. These techniques are practical and quick:

- **Slow breathing.** Breathe in for about four counts and out for about six, two or three times. A longer out-breath helps you settle.
- **A fixed opening routine.** Decide in advance exactly how you will start each section, for example "read the first sentence twice" in Complete the Words. Routines leave less room for worry.
- **Stay in the current item.** The adaptive design means you cannot judge how you are doing from how hard an item feels. Thinking about past items costs attention you need now.
- **Name the problem, then act.** If your mind goes blank in Speaking, say something true and simple, such as "That's a good question; I think the main reason is...", and keep going. A complete, simple answer scores better than silence.

If anxiety seriously affects your daily life or past test performance, consider speaking to a professional. ETS also offers accommodations for documented health-related needs, which must be approved before you register.

<!-- callout: If one section goes badly -->
Every section is scored separately, so a bad section is not a bad test
Your overall band is the average of four section scores. A difficult Listening module or a weak email does not ruin Reading, which is already finished, or Speaking, which has not started. Take two slow breaths at the section break and treat the next section as a fresh start.

<!-- rich_text: The end of the test: report or cancel -->
When you finish, you see your **unofficial Reading and Listening scores**. ETS describes these as unofficial because all scores go through quality checks before release. They rarely change, but they can. You do not see Writing or Speaking scores on the day.

You then choose to **report** or **cancel** your scores. The rules, from ETS's FAQ and score pages:

- **Cancelling cancels everything.** You cannot cancel only one section. Cancelled scores are not reported to you or to any recipients, and the test fee is not refunded.
- **You can change your mind about cancelling.** Cancelled scores can be reinstated through your ETS account within 60 days of the test date, for a fee. ETS says reinstated scores are reported about 3 weeks after it receives the request and payment.
- **You cannot change your mind about reporting.** Once you choose to report, the scores cannot be cancelled.
- **Reported scores go to your free recipients.** If you listed institutions before the test, they will receive the scores from this test date.

<!-- callout: Report or cancel -->
Report your scores unless something went seriously wrong and you listed recipients
Reported scores only reach the institutions you chose, and future reports can show your MyBest scores. Cancelling gives no refund and hides even your good sections. Consider cancelling only if a serious problem clearly ruined the test and you do not want your listed recipients to receive these scores; reinstatement within 60 days remains possible for a fee.

<!-- rich_text: After the test: the next days -->
- **Day 3:** official scores appear in your ETS account, and you get an email. The date is shown at the end of your test and in My TOEFL Home.
- **About 1–2 days later:** a PDF score report is available to download. ETS pages describe this as 1 day and as 24–48 hours after scores arrive. Download it and keep a copy.
- **Paper reports**, if you requested one, are mailed 11–15 days after the test; express shipping in eligible countries usually arrives 2–5 business days after scores are confirmed.
- **Institutions** receive scores according to their delivery method: about 4–8 business days through ETS Data Manager and 8–10 business days through server feeds, with postal mail slower.

**Score review.** If you believe your Writing or Speaking score is wrong, you can request a review of one or both sections for a fee within 30 days of the test. You cannot request a review once you have sent the scores to any institution, and Reading and Listening scores cannot be reviewed. Because free recipients receive scores automatically, a review is only possible if you did not list recipients for this test.

<!-- rich_text: If ETS cancels or reschedules your appointment -->
Occasionally, weather, technical problems or other circumstances force ETS to cancel or reschedule a test appointment, or to cancel scores after a test. ETS explains your options in the *Bulletin* under the cancellation and rescheduling of a test administration.

Two habits protect you:

1. **Keep your contact details current** in your ETS account, especially your phone number and email, so ETS can reach you quickly.
2. **Check your account** the day before and the morning of the test for any changes.

If ETS cancels your scores for a reason such as a testing irregularity, an ID problem or suspected misconduct, its Office of Testing Integrity contacts you by email. ETS says such cancellations usually cannot be appealed, which is one more reason to follow every rule on the day.

<!-- rich_text: Test-day mistakes to avoid -->
1. **A name mismatch** between your ETS account and your ID.
2. **Arriving late** at a test centre, or starting Home Edition check-in at the last minute.
3. **Skipping a section.** If you do not answer at least one item in every section, none of your sections are scored.
4. **A phone or smartwatch within reach** after check-in.
5. **Reading questions aloud** or muttering while thinking during a Home Edition test.
6. **Using headphones at home.** They are not allowed in the Home Edition.
7. **Leaving writing tools enabled**, such as a grammar checker or spell check that opens automatically.
8. **Too much coffee or water** before a two-hour test with no break.
9. **Panicking about the adaptive module.** You cannot tell which module you are in from how it feels.
10. **Cancelling in frustration.** Decide calmly, using the rule above.

<!-- checklist: Test-day kit -->
- Test centre: valid ID that matches my account exactly, plus a second ID if possible.
- Test centre: confirmation of time and building, checked 24 hours before.
- Test centre: plan to arrive 30 minutes early; phone and watch go into storage.
- Home Edition: ID, charged and plugged-in computer, test app installed, equipment check passed today.
- Home Edition: whiteboard or blank sheet in a transparent sleeve, erasable marker.
- Home Edition: second-camera phone or tablet charged, with a sturdy support, if required.
- Home Edition: room private, doors closed, windows covered, desk clear, no food or drink.
- Home Edition: no headphones, smartwatch, jewellery or face mask; ears and face visible.
- Both: meal eaten, bathroom visited, voice warmed up, nothing scheduled straight after.

<!-- rich_text: Frequently asked questions -->
**Can I bring water to the test centre?** Not into the testing room, unless you have an approved health accommodation.

**Can I take a break if I really need one?** There are no scheduled breaks. At home, taking a break can invalidate your test. If you have a medical need, apply for an accommodation before registering.

**What happens if I finish a section early?** Move on when the test allows. Use spare time in Reading or Writing to check answers within the current section.

**Do I need headphones at home?** No. Headsets and earphones are not allowed in the Home Edition; you use your computer's speaker and microphone.

**Can I write notes on paper at home?** Only on a single blank sheet inside a transparent sheet protector, with an erasable marker, or on a small whiteboard.

**Will the proctor talk to me during the test?** The proctor is available by webcam and chat. If they message you, you hear an audio signal from the chat function.

**Can I see my Speaking and Writing scores on the day?** No. Only Reading and Listening scores appear, unofficially, at the end. All official scores arrive 3 days after the test.
