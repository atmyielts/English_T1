## How to use the last two weeks

The final two weeks are not the time to learn new skills. They are the time to **make your existing skills reliable under test conditions**, remove the errors you already know about, and make sure nothing outside the test (ID, equipment, deadlines, sleep) costs you points.

This plan counts down from **Day 14** (two weeks before the test) to **Day 1** (the day before), followed by **test day**. It includes:

- **two full mocks** in the [Schood TOEFL simulator](https://toefl.schood.online/full-exam), one on Day 14 and one on Day 7
- a **review protocol** for each mock
- **targeted repair days** driven by your error log
- the **ETS deadlines** that fall inside these two weeks
- **test-day routines** for a test center and for the Home Edition

Plan about 1.5 to 2.5 hours a day on most days, less in the final three days. If your schedule is tighter, keep the two mock days and the two review days, and shorten the others.

## What you will be able to do {checklist}

- Run two full mocks under realistic conditions and review them properly
- Turn mock results into a short list of final priorities
- Meet every ETS deadline: rescheduling, ID, score recipients
- Prepare a Home Edition room and computer that pass check-in
- Follow a calm, practised routine on test day
- Decide in advance whether to report or cancel your scores

## Ground rules for the final fortnight

1. **No new materials.** Use the simulator, the drills you already know and your error log. New books and new methods create doubt.
2. **Test conditions on mock days.** Same time of day as your test, same desk, same note-taking material, and no pauses between sections: ETS states there are no scheduled breaks in the TOEFL iBT.
3. **Sleep is preparation.** Keep a consistent bedtime for the whole fortnight, especially the last five days.
4. **The error log decides.** After each mock, your two largest error categories become the priorities. Your mood does not.
5. **Protect the strong sections.** Spend short daily sessions on them so they do not slip while you repair the weak ones.

## Before Day 14: the admin check

Do these now, because some cannot be fixed later:

- **Name match:** the name in your ETS account must exactly match the name on your ID, as the 2026–27 Bulletin requires. Check every letter.
- **ID:** a valid, original, physical, government-issued ID with your photo and signature; expired or digital IDs are not accepted. Check the ID rules for your testing location on the ETS website.
- **Home Edition only:** download the TOEFL Test App from the My TOEFL Home dashboard and run the equipment check. If identity verification through a mobile app applies to you, the Bulletin asks you to complete it at least 48 hours before the test.
- **Accommodations:** these must be approved before you register, so if you need them and do not have them, contact ETS now and consider your date.
- **Score recipients:** your fee includes up to four score reports to institutions you choose. List them now so you are not deciding at the last minute.

## Day 14: full mock 1

Take a **full exam in the [simulator](https://toefl.schood.online/full-exam)** at the same time of day as your test. Follow the real order: Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking.

Conditions:

- One sitting, no pauses between sections, phone in another room.
- **Test center candidates:** use pencil and paper for notes.
- **Home Edition candidates:** use only your whiteboard or a sheet in a transparent sheet protector with an erasable marker, and use speakers rather than headphones, because ETS does not allow headsets or earphones at home.

After the mock, do only a **15-minute first look**: write your section bands, how each section felt for time, and the one thing that surprised you. The deep review is tomorrow, when you are rested.

## Day 13: review mock 1

Run the full **review protocol** (described later in this guide) on mock 1. It takes about 90 minutes.

At the end, write on one card:

- your **section bands** and the overall average, rounded the ETS way (to the nearest half band)
- your **two priority categories** for the next week, from the error-log counts
- one **maintenance task** for each strong section

If you are near a target line, check which section offers the cheapest next half band. The [Band 4 to 5](/guide-band-4-to-5-roadmap) and [Band 5 to 6](/guide-band-5-to-6-roadmap) roadmaps explain the averaging arithmetic.

## Day 12: priority 1 repair

Spend about 75 minutes on priority 1 with drills that target the exact category, not the whole section:

- **Reading form or vocabulary errors:** two or three paragraphs from the [Complete the Words Drills](/guide-complete-the-words-drills), timed, with every miss logged.
- **Reading purpose or inference errors:** academic passages with a one-line purpose note per paragraph before answering.
- **Listening detail or reply errors:** short sets of Choose a Response and announcements, labelling reply types.
- **Writing accuracy:** one email with a full two-minute proofread, then a second proofread a day later to see what you missed.
- **Speaking exactness:** ten Listen and Repeat sentences, recorded and checked word by word.

Add 20 minutes of maintenance for one strong section.

## Day 11: priority 2 repair and writing

Repeat yesterday's approach for priority 2 (about 60 minutes), then write **one email in 7 minutes and one discussion post in 10 minutes** under time.

Compare both with the frames in [Email and Discussion Templates](/guide-email-and-discussion-templates):

- Did the email cover every bullet, in the right register?
- Did the discussion post add something new rather than repeat a classmate?
- How many errors did the proofread catch, and how many did you find afterwards?

Log the results. If the second number is high, your proofread order needs fixing.

## Day 10: speaking and academic listening

Speaking comes last in the test, when you are most tired, so it gets its own day:

- **Listen and Repeat (20 minutes):** long sentences, one repetition each, recorded and checked.
- **Interview (30 minutes):** two full four-question interviews using the frameworks in [Interview Answer Frameworks](/guide-interview-answer-frameworks). Record them and score one against the self-scoring checklist.
- **Academic listening (30 minutes):** three academic talks, noting purpose and structure while listening, then answering.

## Day 9: section mock of the weakest section

Take a **timed section** of your weakest section in the [simulator practice area](https://toefl.schood.online/practice), then review it with the same protocol as a full mock.

Compare the result with the same section in mock 1. Do not over-read one result: a section mock taken fresh is often slightly better than the same section at the end of a two-hour test. What you are looking for is whether the **priority categories are shrinking**.

## Day 8: light practice and logistics rehearsal

A lighter day, with one important job:

- **Home Edition:** run the equipment check again **on the same weekday and at the same time of day as your test**, as ETS recommends, because bandwidth varies. Set up the room exactly as on test day (details later) and sit a 20-minute practice set in it.
- **Test center:** confirm the address, plan the route and the travel time, and decide when you must leave home to arrive **at least 30 minutes early**, which the Bulletin requires.

Then do 45 minutes of mixed light practice: one Complete the Words paragraph, two announcements, one email.

## Day 7: full mock 2

Full exam in the [simulator](https://toefl.schood.online/full-exam), under the same conditions as Day 14: same time of day, same notes material, no pauses.

If you are taking the Home Edition, take this mock **in your test room, on your test computer**. If you are going to a test center, take it somewhere unfamiliar if you can, such as a library study room, to practise concentrating in a new space.

Again, only a 15-minute first look today.

## Day 6: review mock 2 and make decisions

Run the review protocol on mock 2, then compare both mocks side by side:

| Reading band | | |
| Listening band | | |
| Writing band | | |
| Speaking band | | |
| Overall (rounded) | | |

Then make two decisions:

- **Keep the date or reschedule.** ETS requires rescheduling or cancelling at least **four full days** before the test (for a Saturday test, by Tuesday), and cancelling by that deadline refunds 50% of the fee. Make this decision today, calmly, using both mocks.
- **Confirm score recipients.** The Bulletin allows you to add, delete or change your free score recipients until **10 p.m. local test center time on the day before the test**. Settle the list today and do not leave it for Day 1.

## Day 5: repair from mock 2

Use mock 2's two largest categories. If they are the same as after mock 1, the drills you used last week are not targeting the real error. Change the drill, not just the amount:

- Reading misses still on purpose questions? Switch from answering questions to writing paragraph functions first.
- Writing errors still in verbs? Make a personal list of your five recurring verb errors and check only those in a fast proofread.
- Speaking answers still short? Use the shrinking-timer routine with a 45-second final recording.

About 90 minutes in total, plus 20 minutes of maintenance.

## Day 4: final timed writing and speaking

A short, sharp session (about 75 minutes):

- One **email** (7 minutes) and one **discussion** (10 minutes), each with the proofread.
- One full **interview** (four questions) and seven **Listen and Repeat** sentences.

Review quickly, log the results and stop. From now on the goal is confidence and rhythm, not volume.

## Day 3: short mixed sets and your top-ten list

- **45 minutes of mixed practice:** one Complete the Words paragraph, one Daily Life set, one academic passage, one conversation and one academic talk.
- **20 minutes:** write your **top-ten error list** from the log: the ten mistakes you are most likely to make, each with its fix in one line ("*a/an before vowel sounds*", "*check -s after he/she/it*", "*no replay in my head: answer and move on*").

Read the list once more in the evening. It will be the last thing you study.

## Day 2: logistics day

Light practice only (30 minutes at most). The rest of the day is preparation:

- **ID** in your bag or on your desk, checked against your ETS account name.
- **Test center:** confirm your appointment details and route; plan to eat before you leave, because there are no scheduled breaks, and prepare any medication you need.
- **Home Edition:** clear the desk and the room; check the camera can be moved for a full room view including the desk surface and the area below it; prepare your whiteboard and marker or your sheet protector; charge the phone or mirror you will use at check-in; close and disable screen-sharing and remote-access software.

## Day 1: the day before

- **20-minute warm-up:** one Complete the Words paragraph, two Listen and Repeat sentences, a 45-second answer to one interview question. Nothing timed, nothing new.
- **Read your top-ten list once.**
- **Final recipients check** before 10 p.m. local test center time, if you did not finish it on Day 6.
- **Pack or set up** everything tonight.
- **Sleep at your usual time.** A late-night review does more harm than good.

## Mock conditions that match the real test

To make your two mocks useful, recreate what ETS describes:

- **Section order:** Reading, Listening, Writing, Speaking.
- **Timing:** ETS lists approximate base times of 30 minutes for Reading, 29 for Listening, 23 for Writing and 8 for Speaking, and advises allowing about two hours in total including directions. Times can vary because Reading and Listening adapt.
- **No scheduled breaks.**
- **Notes:** paper and pencil provided at a test center; only an erasable whiteboard or a sheet in a protector at home.
- **Audio once:** never replay listening audio in a mock.

A mock that breaks these conditions gives you a band that is not comparable to test day.

## The review protocol

Use this for both full mocks and for section mocks. Budget about 90 minutes for a full mock.

1. **Copy results (5 minutes).** Section bands, timing notes and how each section felt.
2. **Reading and Listening misses (30 minutes).** For each miss: the task type, your answer, the correct answer, and one sentence explaining what you should have noticed. Label it with a category.
3. **Writing (20 minutes).** Reread both responses. Mark every error, then check: all bullets covered, new contribution made, register right.
4. **Speaking (20 minutes).** Listen to your recordings if available. Mark Listen and Repeat changes, and judge each interview answer for directness, elaboration and pace.
5. **Count and choose (10 minutes).** Count entries per category. The two largest are your priorities. Write tomorrow's tasks.
6. **One evidence sentence (5 minutes).** Write what changed since the last mock, using only numbers you measured.

Use the [simulator's review pages](https://toefl.schood.online/review) to go back through each item. Remember that Schood mock bands are practice estimates that follow the ETS format and descriptors; your official scores come only from ETS.

## Test-day routine at a test center

**Morning:**
- Eat a normal breakfast at your usual time. Do not try new food or extra caffeine.
- A five-minute warm-up: read a short English paragraph aloud and answer one interview question in your head.

**Arrival and check-in (from the 2026–27 Bulletin):**
- Arrive **at least 30 minutes before** your start time. If you arrive or check in after the start time, you cannot test and the fee is not refunded.
- Bring your **valid ID**. Expect identity checks such as photographs, signature comparison and other verification, and security checks of clothing and pockets.
- **Personal items** go into the storage the staff show you; phones must be switched off and cannot be accessed during the session. The Bulletin limits food and drink in the testing room, so eat beforehand and follow the administrator's instructions on where food, drinks and medication are kept.
- Remove face masks, large jewellery and hair accessories before entering; wedding and engagement rings can stay on.

**In the room:**
- The administrator provides **pencils and scratch paper**. Do not write on anything else, and do not use the paper before the test or between sections. Return all of it at the end.
- There are **no scheduled breaks**, so start the test ready to work for about two hours.

## Test-day routine for the Home Edition

**The room (from ETS's Home Edition requirements):**
- You must be **alone in a closed room**, not in a public place. Cover transparent walls, doors or large open windows, and clear the walls of test-related material.
- Desk or table surface with the computer and keyboard on it, **connected to power**. Sit in a standard chair, not on a bed or sofa. The door should be visible to the proctor.
- The desk and the area around it must be clear of everything not approved: notes, books, phones, cameras.

**The equipment:**
- A desktop or laptop (no tablets), **one monitor, one keyboard**, a licensed operating system (ETS currently lists Windows 11 or higher, or Mac OS X 10.13 or higher; check the equipment check for the latest).
- An internal or external **speaker and microphone; headsets and earphones are not allowed**.
- A camera that can be moved to show the proctor a full view of the room, including the desk surface and the area below it.
- Screen-sharing and remote-access software closed and disabled.

**Check-in (from the Bulletin):**
- You can start check-in **up to 15 minutes before** your scheduled time and **no later than 30 minutes after** it; after that the test is cancelled without a refund.
- Have a **phone or hand-held mirror** for check-in; afterwards the phone goes behind you and out of reach, unless it is your approved second camera.
- Notes only on **one small whiteboard with an erasable marker**, or **one blank sheet inside a transparent sheet protector** with an erasable marker; the sheet must never leave the protector. At the end you will show the proctor that your notes are erased.
- **No food or drink** during the test, no unscheduled breaks, and stay in view of the proctor. Ears and face must stay visible.

## During the test: section reminders

- **Reading:** take the first items carefully; the first stage decides the difficulty of the second. Answer every Complete the Words gap, even with a best guess.
- **Listening:** one play only. Track purpose and structure for talks; label reply types for short exchanges.
- **Writing:** Build a Sentence quickly but carefully, then 7 minutes for the email and 10 for the discussion, each ending with your proofread.
- **Speaking:** in Listen and Repeat, hear the whole sentence, then repeat it in meaning groups. In the interview, answer directly in your first sentence and close at around 40 seconds.
- **If something goes wrong:** at a test center, raise your hand and tell the administrator; at home, follow the proctor's instructions. Do not stop working on your own unless you are told to.

## After the test

What ETS's policies say happens next:

- At the end of the test you see **unofficial Reading and Listening scores** and the date your official scores are expected. They can occasionally change after ETS's quality checks.
- You then choose to **report or cancel** your scores. Cancelling covers all sections, cannot be undone at that moment and gives no refund; cancelled scores can be **reinstated within 60 days** for a fee. Reported scores cannot be cancelled later.
- **Official scores** are posted to your ETS account about **3 days** after the test, and the PDF score report is ready about a day after that.
- Scores are **valid for 2 years**. Your report also shows **MyBest scores**, the highest section scores from valid tests in the last two years.
- You can request a **score review of Speaking and/or Writing** within 30 days of the test, for a fee, but not after scores have been sent to any institution. Reading and Listening cannot be reviewed.
- If you need another attempt, you can test again, but **not more than once in any three-day period**. The [Retake and MyBest Strategy](/retake-and-mybest-strategy) module helps you decide.

## Report or cancel? Decide now

Make this decision before test day, when you are calm, and write it on your card:

- **Report** unless something went clearly and badly wrong (illness, a serious technical problem, a section you could not complete).
- Remember that you will only see **Reading and Listening** unofficial scores when you decide. A lower-than-hoped Reading score is not, on its own, a good reason to cancel: your Writing and Speaking may be stronger than you feel, and MyBest scores can later combine your best sections if your institutions accept them.
- If you set up free score reports to institutions, reporting sends them your scores. If you would rather see the scores before an institution does, decide whether to list recipients in advance or order reports later for a fee.

## Panic protocol {callout}

**If a section goes badly, the next one starts at zero**
Each section is scored separately and then averaged. A difficult Reading section does not lower your Listening, Writing or Speaking scores unless you carry the frustration into them. Between sections, take one slow breath, read your first instruction carefully and treat the next section as a new test.

## Frequently asked questions {faq}

### Should I take a full mock the day before the test?
No. A full mock on Day 1 tires you and can shake your confidence for no gain. The plan's last full mock is on Day 7, with lighter practice afterwards.

### What if my mock 2 result is lower than mock 1?
Look at the error categories, not just the band. One lower mock is normal variation. If both mocks are below your target and the gap is large, use Day 6 to decide whether to keep the date; ETS requires at least four full days' notice to reschedule.

### Can I eat or drink during the Home Edition?
No. ETS's Home Edition requirements state that food and drink are not allowed during the test, and there are no scheduled breaks.

### Can I use headphones for the Home Edition?
No. ETS requires an internal or external speaker and a microphone that is not part of a headset. Practise your listening mocks through speakers too.

### When can I send scores to more institutions?
Up to four free score reports are included if you choose recipients before the deadline. After that, you can order additional score reports through your ETS account for a fee.
