## What the last two weeks are for
Two weeks is not enough time to learn new areas of the test, and that is not the goal. The final 14 days have three jobs:

1. **Rehearse** the exact test experience until it feels routine.
2. **Remove** the most frequent remaining error types, using what your last mocks show.
3. **Protect** your energy, sleep and logistics so that nothing outside the questions costs you points.

This guide gives a day-by-day schedule built around **three full practice tests**, a **forensic review protocol**, and **test-eve and test-day routines**. It assumes you have already worked through most of the public modules; if you have not, the [Test-Day Guide](/test-day-guide) and [Pacing and Adaptive Modules](/pacing-and-adaptive-modules) are the first reads.

## Verified facts that shape the schedule
From official College Board pages (see the sources panel):

- The test is **two sections**: Reading and Writing (two 32-minute modules) and Math (two 35-minute modules), **2 hours 14 minutes** of testing with a **10-minute break** between sections.
- Within a module you can move freely, mark questions for review and use the question menu; **when the timer reaches zero you move on, and you cannot return to a module after leaving it.**
- The testing timer can be hidden, and an alert appears when **5 minutes** remain.
- You need your **fully charged testing device** with **Bluebook installed and exam setup completed**, your **admission ticket** (printed copy preferred), and a **physical photo ID**. Bring pencils or pens for scratch work.
- Your admission ticket becomes available **5 days before the test**, after exam setup.
- A calculator is permitted throughout Math; Bluebook includes a built-in Desmos graphing and scientific calculator, and approved handheld calculators may be used.

| Day | Main task | Load |
|---|---|---|

| 13 | Forensic review of test 1 | Medium |
| 12 | Targeted fix: R&W trap family | Medium |
| 11 | Targeted fix: Math trap family + timed modules | Medium |
| 10 | Full practice test 2 | Heavy |
| 9 | Forensic review of test 2 | Medium |
| 8 | Targeted fix + logistics check | Medium |

**Days are counted backwards to the test.** If your test is on a Saturday, Day 14 is the Saturday two weeks before.

## Choosing your three practice tests
- **Test 1 and Test 3:** official full-length Bluebook practice tests you have not taken before. They show the real interface and the official question style.
- **Test 2:** a full mock in the [Schood SAT simulator](https://sat.schood.online), so that you see fresh material and a different question pool.
- If you have already used all the official tests, use the simulator for all three, and spend more of the review on *process* than on content.
- **Do not** use tests you have taken before as score estimates; familiar questions inflate scores.

## Rules for every full practice test
- Start at the **same time of day as your real test**.
- Use the **device you will test on**, plugged in or fully charged.
- **One sitting,** with only the 10-minute break between sections.
- **No phone,** no music, no pausing, no checking answers.
- Use scratch paper and pencils as on test day.
- Mark every guess and every question where you hesitated between two choices.
- Eat the same breakfast and break snack you plan for test day.

## How the review protocol fits into two hours
Reviews in the final two weeks must be thorough but must not eat the whole day. A timed plan for a full-test review:

- **0–10 min:** list every missed, guessed and slow question, by module.
- **10–60 min:** re-solve them untimed, without explanations. Mark the ones you now get right.
- **60–90 min:** read explanations for the rest; assign cause codes; name traps.
- **90–110 min:** write rule sentences, one per entry.
- **110–120 min:** count codes and choose the next two fix targets.

**Rule:** if you run out of time, skip the questions that were *right but slow* before skipping wrong ones.

## Day 14: Full practice test 1
**Task.** Take Test 1 under the rules above.

**After the test (20 minutes, no more).** Write down three things: how your energy felt in each module, where time felt short, and which questions you remember as hard. Do not start the full review today; you are tired and the review will be shallow.

## Day 13: Forensic review of test 1
Use this protocol for every review in the next two weeks.

**The forensic review protocol (2–3 hours):**
1. **List** every missed, guessed and slow question (Math over about two minutes; R&W over about 90 seconds).
2. **Re-solve** each one *before* reading the explanation, untimed. Note whether you could get it right with time.
3. **Read the explanation** and assign one cause code: C (content), P (process), T (time), X (named slip), G (guess-right).
4. **Name the trap** where one exists, using the trap families in [R&W Hard-Module Traps](/guide-rw-hard-module-traps) and [Math Hard-Module Traps](/guide-math-hard-module-traps).
5. **Write a rule sentence** for each item.
6. **Count** codes and traps. The two most frequent become your fix targets for Days 12 and 11.

Log everything in the [SAT Practice notebook](https://practice.sat.schood.online/notebook).

## Day 12: Targeted fix (Reading and Writing)
- **20 minutes:** reread the guide section for your top R&W trap family.
- **40 minutes:** 15–20 hard questions in that skill from the [SAT Practice Bank](https://practice.sat.schood.online/bank), timed at about 70 seconds each.
- **20 minutes:** review; add rule sentences.
- **15 minutes:** clear [Reviews](https://practice.sat.schood.online/reviews).
- **15 minutes:** Math maintenance, 8 mixed medium-hard questions.

## Day 11: Targeted fix (Math) and timed modules
- **40 minutes:** 15–20 hard questions in your top Math trap family. Use the matching move from [Desmos Power Moves](/guide-desmos-power-moves).
- **20 minutes:** review.
- **Evening, 70 minutes:** one timed R&W module (27 questions, 32 minutes) and one timed Math module (22 questions, 35 minutes) in SAT Practice. Count misses in your two target areas only.

## Day 10: Full practice test 2
Take **Test 2 (simulator mock)** under full rules. Afterwards, write the same 20-minute note as on Day 14, plus one extra line: *did the target errors from test 1 appear again?*

## Day 9: Forensic review of test 2
Run the full protocol. Then compare the two tests:

- **Codes that appeared in both tests** are your real patterns. These become the fix targets for Day 8.
- **Codes that appeared once** may be noise; note them but do not chase them.
- **Section that dropped:** check whether the misses cluster at the end of modules (time or fatigue) or are spread out (content or process).

## Day 8: Targeted fix and logistics check
**Practice (90 minutes):** two short hard-only sets (15 questions each) on the repeated patterns from Day 9, with full review.

**Logistics (30 minutes):**
- Check your test-day device meets the device requirements, and that Bluebook is updated.
- Check your **photo ID** is valid and physical.
- If you plan to use a handheld calculator, confirm it is an approved, non-CAS model and replace its batteries. Remember that Bluebook's Desmos calculator is always available in Math.
- Confirm your test centre address and plan the journey.

## Day 7: Full practice test 3
Your **final full test**. Take it exactly as you will take the real one, from wake-up time to breakfast to break snack. This is a dress rehearsal as much as a score check.

**Important:** whatever the score, do not change your plan dramatically now. Use it to choose the final rule sheet, not to start new topics.

## Day 6: Review test 3 and build your rule sheet
- Run the forensic protocol, but cap it at two hours.
- **Build a one-page rule sheet:** your ten most useful rule sentences from all three reviews, written by hand. Examples: "Count goal verbs in rhetorical synthesis." "Fill the SPR box when converting to a decimal." "Write the combined equation before using the discriminant."
- You will read this sheet once a day until the test.

## Writing a rule sheet that works
A good rule sheet is short, personal and written as instructions to yourself.

**Good rules** are specific actions tied to a question type:
- "Rhetorical synthesis: count the goal verbs; the answer must do all of them."
- "Cross-text: say what Text 2 accepts before what it rejects."
- "Systems with a parameter: compare coefficient ratios, then constants."
- "Percent changes: multiply, never add."
- "SPR: fill the box when converting to a decimal; check the sign."

**Weak rules** are general wishes: "be careful", "read slowly", "focus". If a rule does not tell you what to *do* on a particular kind of question, rewrite it or remove it.

**Ten is the limit.** You will read the sheet once a day for five days. With more than ten rules, few of them stick.

## Day 5: Exam setup and admission ticket
- **Exam setup:** the College Board says your admission ticket becomes available 5 days before the test, after exam setup in Bluebook. Complete exam setup on your testing device, signing in with your College Board account.
- **Admission ticket:** print it (a printed copy is preferred) and also email it to yourself.
- **Practice (70 minutes):** one timed module per section. Treat them as rhythm practice: smooth pacing, calm review at the end.
- **Read your rule sheet.**

## Day 4: Mixed short sets and re-solves
- **45 minutes:** re-solve every logged question from the last ten days, without notes.
- **30 minutes:** a mixed set of 12 R&W and 10 Math questions at medium and hard difficulty.
- **Read your rule sheet.**
- **Sleep:** start going to bed at the time you need for test-day wake-up, if you have not already.

## Day 3: Rules and Desmos refresh
- **R&W (30 minutes):** boundaries rules, agreement and modifier rules, transition relationships; 15 quick conventions questions.
- **Math (30 minutes):** formulas you use often; the reference sheet available in Bluebook; the five Desmos moves you rely on most, rehearsed once each.
- **Route:** check your travel time to the test centre and plan to leave with a margin. Check your admission ticket for the arrival time and centre details.

## Day 2: Light warm-up and pack your bag
- **20 minutes:** 6 R&W and 6 Math questions you are likely to get right. The goal is confidence and rhythm, not learning.
- **Pack your bag tonight** using the checklist below. Charge your device fully.
- **Read your rule sheet** one last time and put it away.

## Bag checklist {checklist}
- Testing device, fully charged, with Bluebook installed and exam setup completed
- Admission ticket (printed copy preferred)
- Physical photo ID
- Pencils or pens for scratch work
- Approved calculator, if you are bringing one (optional; Desmos is built in)
- Charging cable or portable charger (optional; an outlet is not guaranteed)
- A drink and a snack for the break
- A watch without an audible alarm (optional; Bluebook has a timer)
- Your College Board username and password

## Day 1: The test-eve routine
**What to do**
- A short walk, light exercise, or anything that relaxes you.
- A normal dinner, nothing new or heavy.
- Lay out clothes in layers (rooms can be cold or warm).
- Set two alarms.
- Go to bed at your planned time.

**What not to do**
- No new practice questions. At most, 10 minutes of looking at your rule sheet in the afternoon.
- No full tests, no score predictions, no comparing with friends.
- No late screens; your device is already charged and packed.

**If you cannot sleep.** Rest with the lights off and do not get up to study. The sleep routine you built over the previous nights is what you are relying on.

## Managing nerves in the final fortnight
Nerves often grow as the date approaches. Practical steps that keep them manageable:

- **Rehearsal is the main tool.** Three full tests under real conditions make test day feel familiar. That familiarity is the point of the schedule.
- **Keep the routine boring.** Same wake time, same breakfast, same break snack in every full test and on test day.
- **Limit score-checking.** Look at a mock score once, then work from the review, not from the number.
- **Write worries down.** If a worry keeps returning ("what if my device fails?"), write the plan for it (raise your hand, tell the proctor) and put it away.
- **Move every day.** A short walk on review days is an easy reset.

## Testing away from home
If your test centre is in another city or country:

- **Arrive the day before** if travel is long, and keep Day 1 as the test eve wherever you are.
- **Find the centre location** the evening before if you can, so the morning route is known.
- **Pack the bag before travelling,** and keep the device, ticket and ID in your hand luggage.
- **Check local time** against the arrival time on your admission ticket.

## Test-day morning routine
- Wake at your rehearsed time; eat your rehearsed breakfast.
- **Warm-up (10 minutes):** two easy R&W questions and two easy Math questions you have seen before, solved calmly. This switches your brain into test mode without stress.
- Leave with a margin for travel. Check your bag: device, ticket, ID.
- At the centre, follow the proctor's instructions on phones and other devices.

## During the test: module-by-module routine
**Reading and Writing, module 1.** Settle in on the first few questions; they are among the easier ones in each skill group. Do not rush them; module 1 performance determines your second module.

**Reading and Writing, module 2.** If it feels harder, that is the design, not a sign of failure. Keep the claim-first habit, bank time on conventions, mark and move when stuck.

**The 10-minute break.** Drink, eat your snack, stand up. Do **not** replay R&W questions in your head. Read nothing about the test. Return before the break ends.

**Math, module 1.** Brisk and accurate. Re-read every SPR entry before moving on.

**Math, module 2.** Read the last sentence of long questions first. Use your Desmos moves. Reserve the last few minutes for flagged items.

**Throughout:** answer every question. The College Board's assessment framework says there is no guessing penalty and recommends answering every question as well as you can; a blank cannot earn credit.

## If something goes wrong
- **A question you cannot solve:** choose your best answer, mark it, move on. One question is never worth three.
- **Panic:** stop for ten seconds, breathe out slowly, read the question's last sentence again. Then work the first step you can see.
- **Device trouble:** raise your hand and tell the proctor immediately. Do not try to fix it alone.
- **Calculator failure:** use Bluebook's Desmos calculator, which is available throughout Math.
- **A module feels terrible:** how a module feels is not a reliable guide to your score, especially in a harder second module, which is meant to be challenging. Leave it behind at the module boundary.

## After the test
- Rest. Do not rework questions from memory; you cannot change anything now.
- Check your College Board account for score information when it is released.
- When scores arrive, read the Knowledge and Skills section of the score report before deciding anything.
- If you plan to retake, read [Retake and Superscore Strategy](/retake-and-superscore-strategy) and choose the matching Schood plan.

## Adjusting the schedule
- **Only 10 days left?** Drop test 2 and Day 9; keep tests 1 and 3, both reviews and the final five days.
- **Only 7 days left?** One full test on Day 7, review on Day 6, then follow Days 5–1 as written.
- **Test on a school day?** Keep the full tests on weekends and move fix days to weekday evenings (60 minutes each).
- **Very anxious?** Keep all three tests, as repetition is the strongest anxiety treatment, but shorten fix days to 60 minutes and add a daily walk.

## If a mock score drops in the final week
A lower score on test 3 than on test 2 is unsettling, but it is not a reason to change course. Scores vary between tests; College Board score reports include a score range alongside each score to show how much a score would likely vary on another test under identical conditions. Before reacting, check the review:

- **Did the misses come from new trap families?** If so, add them to your rule sheet and do one short targeted set on Day 4.
- **Did they come from fatigue or time?** If the misses cluster at the end of modules, check your sleep, breakfast and break routine on that day. Fix the routine, not the content.
- **Did they come from slips?** If X codes rose, run the zero-careless protocol in the remaining timed modules.
- **Was the test harder or unfamiliar?** Different practice tests vary. Judge by the trend of all three tests, not the last one alone.

**What not to do:** add an extra full test in the last four days, start a new topic, or study late. Each of those makes the real test more tiring without making you more prepared.

## A sample day plan for school weeks
If you are at school during the final fortnight, fit the schedule around lessons. A typical weekday on a fix day:

| Time | Task | Minutes |
|---|---|---|
| Before school | Read your rule sheet (from Day 6 onward) | 5 |
| After school | Targeted hard set in SAT Practice | 40 |
| Short break | Snack, walk, no screens | 15 |
| Early evening | Review the set; rule sentences | 25 |
| Evening | Clear due Reviews | 10 |
| Bedtime | Same time every night | – |

**Weekends** carry the full practice tests and the long reviews. If a full test must move to a weekday, take it on a day with no school, or swap it with the following fix day.

## Food, water and comfort on test day
Keep everything familiar:

- **Breakfast** you have eaten before every full practice test, not something new.
- **Water and a light snack** for the break, as allowed by the testing rules.
- **Layers of clothing** so you can adjust to the room.
- **A watch without an audible alarm** if you like to see time without the on-screen timer (optional; Bluebook has its own timer).

## Final checklist {checklist}
- Three full practice tests taken under real conditions, each reviewed forensically
- Rule sheet of ten personal rules written by hand
- Exam setup completed and admission ticket printed
- Device charged and meeting requirements; Bluebook updated
- Physical photo ID checked
- Calculator approved with fresh batteries, or the decision to use Desmos only
- Route and arrival plan confirmed
- Bag packed the night before
- Sleep schedule aligned with test-day wake-up for at least four nights

## Summary {callout: The two-week rule}
Rehearse, remove, protect. Three full tests, three forensic reviews, a handwritten rule sheet, and no new material in the last 48 hours.
